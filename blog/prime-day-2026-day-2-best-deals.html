Prime Day 2026 Day 2: The Best Deals Still Available (Plus What to Skip)

Published June 24, 2026 · 5 min read

We're halfway through Prime Day 2026, and here's the truth: some deals are genuinely great, and some are just marketing. Day 1 was a frenzy — lightning deals sold out in minutes, Amazon's servers had hiccups, and a lot of people bought things they didn't need because the countdown timer made them panic.

Day 2 is where the smart shoppers win. The hype has cooled down. Stock has been refreshed. And the deals that are still available are the ones actually worth buying — because if they were junk, they'd have sold out already.

🔥 Pro tip: Before you buy anything on Day 2, check SnappRice. Walmart and Target are running competing sales, and the same item can be $30–50 cheaper at a different store. Always compare.

What's Actually Worth Buying on Day 2

1. Amazon Devices (Echo, Fire TV, Ring)

These are the deepest discounts of any Prime Day. The Echo Dot is down to $19.99 (60% off), Fire TV Stick 4K is $24.99, and Ring doorbells are 40-50% off. These prices won't be beat at Walmart or Target — Amazon controls the pricing on its own hardware. If you've been waiting to smart-up your home, Day 2 is the time.

2. Last-Gen TVs (Not the Latest Models)

The 2025 model TVs are heavily discounted to clear inventory for 2026 models. A 65-inch 4K TV from Samsung or LG that was $1,200 last year is hovering around $550–700 right now. The picture quality is still excellent. The 2026 model is barely different. Don't chase the newest model — save the $500.

3. Back-to-School Supplies

It's only June, but back-to-school is already in full swing. Amazon, Walmart, and Target are all competing on laptops, backpacks, and dorm essentials. A decent Chromebook for $150–200 and a noise-canceling headphone set for $50–80 are excellent deals right now. By August, prices will go back up when demand spikes.

4. Household Essentials (Diapers, Paper Towels, Laundry Pods)

With inflation at 4.2%, every dollar on essentials counts. Prime Day discounts on household brands like Tide, Huggies, and Bounty are usually 20-30% off. But here's the catch — Walmart's Summer Savings sale often beats Amazon on these exact items. Always compare before you stock up.

5. Wireless Earbuds (Samsung, Sony, JBL)

AirPods are getting the hype, but Samsung Galaxy Buds and Sony WF series earbuds are deeply discounted on Day 2. We've seen Galaxy Buds 3 Pro at $139 (down from $249) and Sony WF-1000XM5 at $199 (down from $299). These are legitimate deals, not inflated MSRP games.

What to Skip on Day 2

Fashion & Apparel

Amazon isn't a fashion destination. The "70% off" on unknown clothing brands is almost always a markup game. If you need summer clothes, Target Circle Week has better deals on recognizable brands. Use SnappRice to compare before you buy.

Furniture

Big furniture deals on Prime Day look tempting, but shipping costs and assembly hassle eat into the savings. Plus, Walmart and Target often have the same items for less without the Prime membership requirement.

Generic Electronics Accessories

Cables, chargers, screen protectors, and phone cases from brands nobody has heard of — these are 90% markup games. The "was $39.99, now $9.99" cable was never worth $39.99. Buy these on eBay or from Anker directly.

Day 2 Strategy: How to Shop Smarter

You've survived Day 1. Now here's how to win Day 2:

  • Check restocks early. Amazon refreshes inventory around 6-8 AM EST. The best new deals drop then.
  • Don't trust the "X% claimed" bar. Amazon has been known to create artificial urgency. Take your time.
  • Use the "Keepa" or SnappRice price tracker. See if the "deal" price is actually lower than the average price over the last 6 months.
  • Compare the same item at Walmart and Target. Circle Week and Summer Savings overlap with Prime Day. Don't assume Amazon is cheapest.
  • Buy with a credit card that offers price protection. Some cards will refund the difference if the price drops further within 60-90 days.
