Post-4th of July Sales 2026: The Best Clearance Deals Still Available at Walmart, Target & Best Buy

Published July 6, 2026 · 4 min read

The fireworks are over, the burgers have been grilled, and the 4th of July sales have officially ended. But here's the thing most shoppers don't realize: the real deals happen after the holiday.

Retailers need to clear out seasonal inventory — grills, patio furniture, outdoor gear, and summer electronics — to make room for back-to-school merchandise. That means deep discounts on items that were already marked down for July 4th.

🔥 Key insight: Post-holiday clearance markdowns are often 20–40% deeper than the sale prices themselves. The same grill that was $299 on July 3 could be $179 on July 6. Always compare prices at SnappRice before you buy.

1. Grills & Outdoor Cooking — Up to 50% Off

July 4th is the peak of grilling season, and stores know it. They stock up heavily on grills, smokers, and accessories in June, then need to clear them out by August when back-to-school takes over the sales floor.

This is the best time to buy a premium grill. Look for markdowns on floor models and open-box items at Home Depot, Lowe's, and Walmart. Weber, Traeger, and Blackstone models that were $500–$800 during the holiday can often be found for $300–$500 in the days after.

Check current grill prices across multiple retailers at SnappRice before you pull the trigger.

2. Patio Furniture — Deep Discounts on Floor Models

Patio furniture follows the same pattern as grills. Stores need to clear summer inventory to make room for fall merchandise. Look for:

  • Outdoor dining sets — 30–50% off at Target and Walmart
  • Adirondack chairs and lounge sets — Best deals at Lowe's and Home Depot
  • Umbrellas and shade solutions — Often bundled at clearance prices
  • Fire pits — Surprisingly good deals in July since stores want the floor space

3. TVs and Electronics — The Post-Holiday Letdown

TV deals during 4th of July are usually solid, but the post-holiday period is mixed. Some models go back up to full price, while others — especially the models that didn't sell well — get additional markdowns.

Your best bet is to check prices on specific models you're interested in. A 65-inch 4K TV that was $499 during the sale might still be available at that price if inventory is high, or it might have dropped even further if stores are clearing last year's model.

📌 Smart strategy: Don't assume the sale is over. Many retailers extend their 4th of July pricing for 48–72 hours after the holiday, especially on items with high inventory. Check SnappRice to see who still has the best price.

4. Summer Clothing & Swimwear — Clearance Season Starts

July is the beginning of end-of-summer clearance for clothing. Swimsuits, shorts, sundresses, and sandals all go on deep discount as stores transition to fall collections. Target, Walmart, and Amazon are the best places to check.

This is also a great time to buy kids' summer clothes for next year — if you can guess their size. The discounts are often 60–75% off by mid-July.

5. Outdoor Toys & Pool Gear

Inflatable pools, water slides, outdoor games, and pool accessories see massive markdowns after the 4th. Retailers don't want to store this bulky inventory for another year. You can often find inflatable pools at 50–70% off starting the week after July 4th.

6. What NOT to Buy Right Now

Not everything is a deal in early July. Here's what to skip:

  • Back-to-school supplies — Wait until late July / early August for the best prices
  • New laptops for college — Better deals during Amazon Prime Day or late July sales events
  • Winter clothing — Still months away from clearance; buy in January
  • Smartphones — New models typically launch in September; current gen prices will drop then

How to Find the Best Post-Holiday Deals

The key to scoring post-4th of July clearance deals is comparison shopping. One store might have grills at 40% off while another still lists them at full price. Here's how to shop smart:

  1. Use SnappRice to search for the specific product you want and compare prices across Amazon, Walmart, Target, Best Buy, and eBay
  2. Check open-box and clearance sections at major retailers — these aren't always indexed by search engines
  3. Set price alerts on items you're watching — prices can drop again as stores get more aggressive with clearance
  4. Visit stores in person — floor models and display units often have additional markdowns that aren't reflected online

💡 Bottom line: The week after any major holiday is one of the best times to shop for seasonal items. Retailers are motivated to clear inventory, and shoppers who know where to look can save hundreds. Start your search at SnappRice and compare before you buy.
