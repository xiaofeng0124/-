Amazon vs Walmart vs Target 2026: Which Retailer Actually Has the Lowest Prices?

Published July 15, 2026 · 7 min read · Updated for 2026

Every shopper wants the same thing: the lowest possible price. But when you're choosing between Amazon, Walmart, and Target — the three largest retailers in America — it's not always obvious which one is cheaper. The answer, as we found after comparing hundreds of prices in July 2026, is it depends on what you're buying.

Each retailer has clear strengths and weaknesses when it comes to pricing. Some categories you'll save big at Walmart. Others, Amazon's algorithm-driven pricing can't be beat. And Target? It wins on experience and convenience — but sometimes at a premium.

The Head-to-Head: Pricing by Category

We tracked prices across 12 common shopping categories in July 2026 to see which retailer consistently wins on price. Here's the breakdown:

Electronics & Gadgets — Winner: Amazon

Amazon dominates electronics pricing thanks to its dynamic pricing algorithm, which adjusts prices hundreds of times per day based on competitors, demand, and inventory levels. During our July 2026 check:

  • MacBook Air M4: $949 at Amazon vs $979 at Best Buy vs $999 at Target (with $50 gift card)
  • AirPods Pro 2: $179 at Amazon vs $189 at Walmart vs $199 at Target
  • Samsung 65" QLED TV: $997 at Amazon vs $1,047 at Walmart vs $1,099 at Target
  • Apple iPad 10th gen: $299 at Amazon vs $329 at Best Buy vs $349 at Target

Best Buy is actually competitive here — especially on open-box items — but Amazon's pricing is consistently 5–10% lower on most electronics, with the gap widening during sales events.

📌 Note on electronics: While Amazon usually has the lowest price, Best Buy offers the best post-purchase support. If you're buying a $1,000+ laptop, the extra $30–$50 at Best Buy might be worth it for their Geek Squad support and easy returns. Check current prices at SnappRice and decide.

Groceries & Household Essentials — Winner: Walmart

This is Walmart's territory. The retail giant's supply chain efficiency means everyday low prices on groceries, cleaning supplies, and household items. In our comparison:

  • A standard grocery basket of 20 common items: $47.23 at Walmart vs $52.18 at Target vs $58.47 at Amazon Fresh
  • Dish soap (Dawn, 28oz): $3.97 at Walmart vs $4.49 at Target vs $5.24 at Amazon
  • Paper towels (Bounty, 12-pack): $21.97 at Walmart vs $24.99 at Target vs $27.49 at Amazon
  • Laundry detergent (Tide, 150oz): $14.97 at Walmart vs $16.79 at Target vs $18.99 at Amazon

Walmart's grocery pickup is free on orders over $35, making it even more convenient. Target comes closer on price for store-brand items (Up&Up vs Great Value), but Walmart still edges ahead on national brands.

Home Goods & Furniture — Winner: Target

Target wins this category, but not necessarily on price alone — it's the combination of style, quality, and value. Target's in-house brands (Threshold, Project 62, Opalhouse) offer modern designs at approachable prices. When we compared:

  • Decorative throw pillows: $15–$25 at Target vs $18–$35 at Amazon (comparable quality)
  • Bath towels (6-piece set): $24.99 at Target (Threshold) vs $29.99 at Amazon (comparable quality)
  • Table lamps: $19.99 at Target vs $16.99 at Walmart (Walmart wins on price, but Target's styling is better)

Amazon is competitive on basics like bedding and towels, but Target's design aesthetic and in-store experience make it the preferred choice for home decor. Walmart is cheapest for functional items but lacks the style factor.

Clothing & Apparel — Winner: Target (for value) / Amazon (for selection)

Target's clothing brands — particularly Cat & Jack (kids), A New Day (women), and Goodfellow & Co (men) — offer excellent quality at reasonable prices. A women's dress runs $25–$40, and kids' basics start at $5. The quality-to-price ratio is the best among the three.

Amazon has a much wider selection — millions of clothing items vs thousands at Target — but quality can be inconsistent. Walmart's clothing is the cheapest (t-shirts from $3.98) but the fabrics and fits are generally lower quality.

School Supplies — Winner: Walmart (price) / Target (quality)

For basic school supplies, Walmart is undeniably cheaper. A full K-5 supply list costs about $30 at Walmart vs $42 at Target. But Target's supplies feel more durable — their spiral notebooks have thicker covers, and their markers last longer. For college students who need supplies to last a semester, the extra spend at Target is often worth it.

Toys & Games — Winner: Amazon (selection + price)

Amazon has the widest selection and typically the best prices on toys and games, thanks to its massive third-party marketplace. Target and Walmart match prices on popular items (Lego, Hot Wheels, Nintendo) but Amazon wins on variety, especially for niche toys and educational games. During holiday season, all three are highly competitive — last year we saw price differences of less than $2 on most popular toys.

When to Buy at Each Retailer

Buy from Amazon when:

  • You're shopping for electronics, laptops, or headphones
  • You need something delivered fast (Prime 1-day shipping)
  • You want the widest selection and don't need to see items in person
  • It's Prime Day (mid-year) or Black Friday

Buy from Walmart when:

  • You're stocking up on groceries, household essentials, or diapers
  • You need basic school supplies in bulk
  • Price is your #1 concern and you don't care about brand names
  • You want free curbside pickup on a budget

Buy from Target when:

  • You're decorating a dorm room or apartment
  • You need quality clothing at reasonable prices
  • You want a pleasant in-store experience (clean, well-lit, organized)
  • You have a Target RedCard (5% off everything, always)
  • It's Target Circle Week (July) or Deal Days
